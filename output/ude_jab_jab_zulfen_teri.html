Ude Jab Jab Zulfen Teri

A playful, flirtatious banter between a young man and woman in a rustic, village setting. The song is a back-and-forth exchange where the man playfully teases the woman about her beauty and its effect on men, and she cleverly responds, matching his wit.

Movie / Album Naya Daur (1957)
Singers Mohammed Rafi, Asha Bhosle
Actors
Dilip KumarVyjayanthimala
Composer O. P. Nayyar
Lyricist Sahir Ludhianvi

Vocabulary Guide

Zulfen Zool-fen
Hair, specifically locks or tresses of hair.
Kanwariyon Kun-waa-ri-yon
Young, unmarried girls/maidens.
Machle Much-lay
To flutter, act restless, or get excited/agitated.
Jind meriye Jind may-ree-yay
A Punjabi term of endearment meaning 'Oh my life' or 'my soul'.
Chikne Chik-nay
Literally 'smooth' or 'glossy'. In this context, it colloquially means handsome or well-groomed.
Phisle Phis-lay
To slip. Used metaphorically here to mean losing one's focus or becoming distracted by beauty.
Rut Root
Season (often used poetically to refer to a season of love or spring).
Beriyon / Ber Bay-ri-yon / Bayr
Berries / Jujube fruit (Ziziphus mauritiana). A common fruit in rural India.
Sadke Sud-kay
To sacrifice oneself, or to be utterly devoted to someone/something out of love.
Yaar Yaar
Friend or beloved/lover.
Basta Bus-taa
Resides, lives, or settles.
Goriye Go-ree-yay
A fair-skinned or beautiful young woman.
Chhedenge Chhay-deng-gay
Will tease, bother, or provoke.
Nagan Naa-gun
A female serpent/cobra. Often used metaphorically in Indian poetry to describe a woman's graceful, swaying walk.
Jogi Jo-gee
An ascetic, mystic, or snake-charmer. Here used playfully to imply someone will be captivated by her serpent-like walk.
Sajna Suj-naa
Beloved, sweetheart, or husband.
